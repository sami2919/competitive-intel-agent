## Strategy in plain English

Gusto's marketing strategy centers on being the simple, all-in-one payroll/HR/benefits platform for small businesses, backed by strong satisfaction proof points [CAN-004] and a scale claim of 500,000+ businesses [CAN-001]. Their most proven ad angles are migration from competitors [CAN-008] and ease-of-use/reliability messaging [CAN-009], both running long enough to be considered durable winners. They're simultaneously building a wedge product (Gusto Solo) aimed at solopreneurs and S-corp owners with tax-savings messaging [CAN-007], and are experimenting with AI (Cofounder) as a newer, less-proven differentiator [CAN-014]. The core narrative is consistent: payroll/tax/compliance complexity, handled simply, for a small-business audience that has not yet outgrown Gusto's ceiling.

## Rippling-relevance

### Where they win / Where we win

- **Where they win:** Simplicity and ease-of-use as the dominant brand promise, proven across 2 sources with creative running 1420 days [CAN-009]. **Where we win:** Rippling's compound platform architecture scales past the point where "simple" tools hit a ceiling — multi-state, multi-entity, global. [30–200 employee migration segment]
- **Where they win:** Category-defining "all-in-one payroll/HR/benefits" positioning, corroborated across 5 sources [CAN-002]. **Where we win:** Rippling's "all-in-one" extends to IT and Finance, not just HR — a structurally larger platform claim. [Mid-market / scaling SMB segment]
- **Where they win:** Proven migration/switching campaign angle already running 1309+ days [CAN-008]. **Where we win:** Rippling can run the same play in reverse, specifically targeting companies that migrated TO Gusto early and have since outgrown it. [30–200 employee migration segment]
- **Where they win:** Deep solopreneur/S-corp wedge via Gusto Solo, cross-channel winner [CAN-007]. **Where we win:** Rippling doesn't need to compete here — this segment self-selects out of Rippling's ICP, freeing focus for headcount growth stages Gusto's Solo doesn't serve. [Solopreneur segment — concede, don't contest]

### Landmines to plant

- "What happens to your payroll workflow when you open a second state?" — traces to Gusto's core simplicity positioning [CAN-009], which is not proven to extend cleanly to multi-state complexity.
- "How does your HR platform talk to your IT provisioning and finance systems today?" — traces to Gusto's HR-only platform scope [CAN-002], which has no IT/Finance layer.
- "As you've added international contractors, how are you handling multi-currency payments beyond USDC/Wise?" — traces to Gusto's international contractor payment positioning [CAN-012], which is narrow (contractor-only, not full international payroll/EOR).

### Objection handling

- **Objection:** "Gusto is rated #1 on G2 for satisfaction." Reframe: G2 satisfaction is strongest among small, single-state businesses [CAN-004] — ask what their review profile looks like among 100+ employee, multi-state customers specifically.
- **Objection:** "Gusto has unlimited payroll runs at no extra cost." Reframe: Confirm Rippling's parity on unlimited runs [CAN-006], then pivot to the broader platform question — payroll frequency isn't the differentiator, platform breadth is.
- **Objection:** "Gusto's migration process is easy — they've marketed this for years." Reframe: Their switching campaign [CAN-008] is optimized for *from-competitor-to-Gusto* migration; ask what their playbook looks like for growing OUT of Gusto, which is the actual objection Rippling should own.

### Campaign angles

1. **"The platform you won't outgrow" — direct migration campaign.** Rationale: Gusto's proven migration angle [CAN-008] and simplicity ceiling [CAN-009] create a clear counter-positioning opportunity. [30–200 employee migration segment]
2. **Multi-state/multi-entity compliance complexity contrast.** Rationale: Gusto's tax-filing pillar [CAN-003] is strong but scoped to standard federal/state/local — no evidence of multi-entity depth. [Scaling SMB / mid-market]
3. **Compound platform (HR+IT+Finance) vs. HR-only.** Rationale: Gusto's all-in-one claim [CAN-002] is HR-scoped; Rippling's platform breadth is a structural, not just messaging, advantage. [Mid-market segment]

## How to read this brief

- **Verdict** — BLUF (bottom line up front): the 3-5 key judgments, each with
  a confidence label and a concrete '→ For Rippling:' action. If a judgment
  implies no action, it doesn't appear.
- **Strategy in plain English** — a one-paragraph synthesis of the competitor's
  current strategy, restating only body-eligible (confidence >= 0.5) claims with
  citations. Synthesis, not new evidence.
- **What's Winning** — claims that persist (an ad running 90+ days) or are
  corroborated across 2+ independent channels. Each carries a deterministic
  `winning=NN/100` score (corroboration 40 + persistence 30 + recency 30) and
  ends with a 'why it likely persists' clause tagged as inference, not measured.
- **What Looks Like a Test** — ads under 90 days old and single-channel.
  These are HYPOTHESES ('may be testing...'), not conclusions.
- **What Changed Recently** — strategic shifts only (positioning, pricing,
  ICP, messaging, leadership/funding), not product-launch inventory.
- **The three-way distinction** — strategic shift (changes who they target or
  how they frame themselves) vs product launch (new feature in the existing
  story, relegated to a supporting note) vs marketing test (new, single-channel
  ad variant, belongs in 'What Looks Like a Test'). Segment moves are labeled
  new-ICP vs extension-of-existing-motion; press-driven shifts are flagged as
  'appears to / directionally', not settled fact.
- **Unverified signals** — confidence below 0.5. Raw signal; never treated as fact.
- **Rippling-relevance** — a battlecard: where they win / where we win (honest
  concessions included), landmines (discovery questions), objection handling,
  and ranked campaign angles — each backed only by confidence >= 0.5 evidence
  and tagged with its target segment.
- **Campaign test hypotheses** — ranked 'we believe X because [evidence]' bets
  for Rippling's growth team, derived from ad longevity/corroboration signals.
  Hypotheses to test, never conclusions.
- **Confidence labels** — deterministic ICD-203-style wording mapped from the
  rubric: high (2+ independent sources) / moderate (single primary) /
  moderate-low (single secondary) / low (inferred). Ad longevity labels:
  proven (90d+) / sustained (45-89d) / maturing (14-44d) / just launched (<14d).

Grounding: every `[CLM-xxx]` / `[CAN-xxx]` citation maps to a real ledger claim
(no hallucinated IDs). Sub-gate (confidence < 0.5) claims appear only in
'What Looks Like a Test', 'Campaign test hypotheses', or 'Unverified signals',
framed as hypotheses; body-eligible (>= 0.5) claims may NOT hide in the
Unverified appendix. Validation is deterministic Python, not model self-report.
